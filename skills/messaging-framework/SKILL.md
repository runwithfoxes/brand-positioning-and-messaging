# Messaging framework

You are building the messaging framework with the person you are talking to. It is what the writer
says. The positioning statement decides what the copy is allowed to be, and nothing in this framework
may contradict it.

## Check what exists first

The brand documents live in a folder called `brand/` inside the folder the person is working in.
Read all five: `audience.md`, `competitors.md`, `proof.md`, `positioning-statement.md` and
`tone-of-voice.md`.

**It is built from the five above, not from scratch.** If any are missing, say which, and offer to
build them first with the brand-foundations, positioning-statement or tone-of-voice skill.

## How to run it

**One question at a time.** Draft from the brand files, then ask about the gaps one by one. Never
send a list of questions.

**Never invent.** A pillar's proof comes from `proof.md` or from what they tell you, with its
source. If there is none, write `[not known yet]` under the pillar and say so. A pillar without its
proof is a slogan.

**Follow up once when an answer is generic,** then take what you get and move on.

## What is in it

- **The one-line value proposition.**
- **What it does** and **what it means to the customer.** Different things, both needed. What it
  does is the mechanics. What it means is what changes for the person, and different segments often
  care about different parts of it.
- **Three or four pillars.** For each: the claim, the proof underneath it with a source, and what
  the competition says instead.
- **The grid.** Pillars down the side, the segments from `audience.md` across the top, and in each
  cell the thing that pillar says to that segment, in the situation where it lands. Under the grid,
  one line per segment on its buying situation: when that segment is actually in the market.
- **Proof under each pillar,** with its source. Mark anything that is a capability rather than a
  number as such.
- **What you will never say.** The category clichés (the entry-fee list in `competitors.md`), and
  anything you cannot prove, including anything marked `[unverified]` in `proof.md`.
- **A pointer to the tone of voice.** It lives in its own document, `tone-of-voice.md`, not inside
  this one.
- **For the writer reading this file.** Every piece of copy is one pillar, aimed at one segment, in
  one buying situation. Name all three before drafting. If you cannot, the brief is not ready. A
  pillar without its proof is a slogan, so carry the number. Do not write a fourth pillar. If a
  draft needs one, bring it back rather than inventing it.

## What to hand back

The whole document in one message, with a line at the top saying when it was made, that it came
from an interview, and which files it was built from. Save it to `brand/messaging-framework.md`
once they are happy with it. If you cannot write files, give it in one message to copy.

When this is done the brand pack is complete. Tell them the writer skill can now write emails, blog
posts and web pages from it, and that the writer will read these six files every time.

## Worked example

`example-kite.md`, beside this file, is a finished messaging framework for a fictional insurance
brand called Kite, with three pillars, a three-by-three grid and proof with sources. Read it for the
form. Never copy Kite's claims or numbers. They are invented.
